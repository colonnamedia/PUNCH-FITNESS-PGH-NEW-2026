How PUNCH Uses Boxing

Find Your Pace.
Then Turn It Up.

Think of changing your effort during a boxing class like changing pace during cardio. Begin with controlled movement, settle into a rhythm, move more through your legs and core, and work through short bursts before recovering.

Walk

Get Moving
Learn the punches and begin shifting your weight and moving at a comfortable pace. It’s your starting point for getting your whole body involved.

Jog

Build a Steady Effort
Keep the combinations flowing and add more stepping. Your breathing picks up as you maintain a rhythm you can work with.

Run

Challenge Your Stamina
Move your feet through the combinations, drive from your legs, and turn through your core as the pace increases.

Sprint

Give a Strong Burst
Put your legs, hips, core, and arms into a short, powerful round. Then recover and get ready to go again.

Your coach changes the pace. You control your effort.

First-Class Questions

Start With Zero Experience.

Do I need boxing experience?

No. Your coach introduces the basics and guides you through the rounds. Arrive about 10 minutes early for your first visit.

Is this contact boxing?

No. This is boxing for fitness. You punch heavy bags and work with coaches on mitts, without sparring or punching other members.

Is boxing really a cardio workout?

Yes. Punching combinations, footwork, rotation, and changes in pace challenge your heart and lungs while involving your legs, core, and upper body. You can adjust the effort as your fitness develops.

What do I need for my first class?

Wear comfortable workout clothes, bring water, and arrive early. Loaner gloves are available for your first visit.
