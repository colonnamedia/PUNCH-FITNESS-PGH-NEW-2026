Build Your Training Week

Start Where You Are. Build From There.

Your week does not have to look like someone else’s. Match your food, hydration, sleep, and recovery habits to how often you train—then build gradually.

1Day / Week

Start The Routine

One workout is a real starting point. You do not need a strict diet to begin, but if results are the goal, consistent food choices help. Over the next three months, aim for two days when your schedule allows.

2Days / Week

Build Consistency

Two workouts begin to create a routine. Plan your food and hydration on training days so you arrive ready and recover well afterward.

3Days / Week

Create Momentum

This is where progress can start to feel more consistent—especially when everyday eating, hydration, sleep, and recovery support the work you are doing.

4Days / Week

Support The Demand

Four workouts raise the week’s recovery demands. Harder sessions can create more EPOC—or “afterburn”—so regular food, hydration, sleep, and recovery matter more. That does not mean every workout needs to be your hardest.

5–6Active Days / Week

Fuel An Active Week

This may combine PUNCH classes with basketball, pickleball, incline walking, or other activity. Keep nutrition and hydration consistent throughout the week to support energy, recovery, performance, and muscle maintenance.

Maximize results. Protect your energy. Support your recovery.

Not every workout should feel the same. Some days build. Some days maintain. Some days help you recover. Consistency matters more than making every session maximal.

What Success Looks Like

Train For More Than Calories.

Success is not just finishing a hard workout. It is becoming stronger, recovering faster, breathing more efficiently, and building a body that handles more without every session having to feel maximal.

01

Cardiovascular Conditioning

Cardio trains your heart and lungs to deliver oxygen more efficiently. Better conditioning helps you sustain effort, recover between rounds, and handle everyday activity with less fatigue.

02

Strength Without The Bulk Myth

Getting stronger is not the same as suddenly getting bulky. Strength training supports muscle, joint stability, body composition, and confidence. At PUNCH, the goal is training that matches what you want to achieve.

03

Intervals, Effort And Recovery

HIIT alternates demanding work with recovery. That repeated rise and fall teaches you to produce effort, regain control, and go again. Some days push harder; other days build skill or maintain the routine.

EPOC, In Plain English

After a challenging interval or strength session, your body may continue using extra oxygen and energy while it returns toward its resting state. This is excess post-exercise oxygen consumption—the “afterburn” effect. Its size and duration vary with the workout, so treat it as a useful bonus, not a promise or a replacement for consistent training and nutrition.

Basic Nutrition Truths

One Choice Is Not The Problem. The Pattern Matters.

A hamburger, milkshake, or bowl of ice cream does not make your body “start over.” But when exceptions happen often, they become the routine—and that routine may stop matching the result you want.

Skip The Reset Myth

Your Results Reflect What You Repeat.

There is no universal one-week, one-month, or age-based reset clock. Progress comes from the eating, activity, sleep, and recovery habits you can repeat over time.

One meal does not erase progress. One workout does not complete it.
01

Build A Reliable Base

Choose meals built around protein, produce, fiber-rich foods, and portions that support your goal and training.

02

Know What Adds Up

Added sugar, saturated fat, sodium, and oversized portions can make a goal harder when they show up frequently—not because one meal flips a fat-storage switch.

03

Fuel The Work

As training demands rise, regular food and hydration help support energy, muscle maintenance, performance, and recovery.

04

Plan Real Life

Enjoyable food can fit. Plan it honestly instead of treating every choice as either perfect or ruined.

Good To Know

Nutrition Coaching Questions.

Is this a prescribed diet or medical nutrition program?

No. PUNCH provides general wellness education, practical suggestions, habit coaching, and accountability. We do not diagnose or treat medical conditions, prescribe therapeutic diets, or replace care from a physician or registered dietitian.

Do I have to be a PUNCH member?

No. Member and non-member coaching options will be available, with separate pricing when enrollment opens.

What should I track?

Track your wake time, meal and snack timing, what and approximately how much you ate, hydration, daily activity level, and any useful notes. The goal is an honest baseline—not a perfect-looking week.

Will you tell me exactly what to eat?

We focus on education, suggestions, routines, and accountability. Your PUNCH Playbook will identify practical habits to work on. For medical nutrition therapy or an individualized therapeutic diet, we will encourage you to work with an appropriately licensed healthcare professional.

Important: PUNCH nutrition coaching is for general education and wellness support only. It is not medical advice, medical nutrition therapy, diagnosis, or treatment. Consult a physician or registered dietitian for medical conditions, allergies, eating disorders, pregnancy, medications, or individualized therapeutic nutrition needs.
